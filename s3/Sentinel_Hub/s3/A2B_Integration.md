## Part 3: Pressure (A) to Price (B)—The Causal Mechanism

### How Pressure in Table A Becomes Price in Table B

The causal mechanism has six stages:

#### Stage 1: Information Asymmetry (Table A)

**Starting point:** Some market participants have information that others lack.

Examples:
- A large fund has research indicating upward bias for a stock
- A central bank is about to signal rate increases
- A company has better-than-expected earnings
- A news event is about to break

Result: Information creates directional bias. Some participants know where the market "should" go.

#### Stage 2: Directional Pressure Builds (Table A)

Informed participants begin to act on their information advantage:
- Buy orders accumulate at offer (aggressive buying)
- Sell orders accumulate at bid (aggressive selling)
- Order book imbalance develops
- One side of the book becomes heavier than the other
- **Pressure gradient forms**

This is entirely in Table A. Price hasn't moved yet. But pressure is building.

#### Stage 3: Pressure Propagates (Table A)

Pressure gradient drives information flow through the microstructure:

- Informed traders' orders move the bid-ask midpoint
- Market makers adjust inventory to manage risk
- Other informed traders recognize the pressure, join in
- Momentum traders see order flow patterns, follow
- Uninformed traders are swept along by flow

**Transfer Entropy measures this propagation.** How much does the order book's pressure state (informed) inform the subsequent price movement (uninformed)? In quiet, clarity-gated regimes: TE ≈ 0.498 bits/trade.

Pressure cascades through the network of market participants. Information spreads directionally.

#### Stage 4: Clarity and Regime Conditions (A ↔ B Boundary)

Whether the pressure propagates cleanly or gets obscured depends on clarity:

**High clarity (coherent pressure):**
- All information points the same direction
- No competing forces
- Pressure propagates efficiently
- Price discovery is clean
- TE is measurable (0.498 bits/trade)

**Low clarity (fragmented pressure):**
- Information is mixed, conflicting
- Competing forces offset pressure
- Pressure propagates slowly or not at all
- Price discovery is chaotic
- TE is degraded (0.05-0.15 bits/trade)

Clarity gates how effectively Table A pressure translates into Table B price movement.

#### Stage 5: Price Discovery (Transition A → B)

Informed traders exhaust available liquidity at current prices.
- Bids (or asks) get filled
- Market makers must refresh inventory
- To attract new liquidity, they move prices
- **Price discovery happens: bid-ask midpoint shifts**

This is the transition from Table A (pressure, order flow) to Table B (price, visible movement).

Price discovery is the mechanism by which Table A pressure resolves into Table B expression.

#### Stage 6: Visible Expression (Table B)

Prices now reflect the pressure that built in Table A:

- Higher prices if buy pressure was dominant
- Lower prices if sell pressure was dominant
- Visible momentum is now detectable
- Traditional analysis sees "momentum" and tries to trade it
- But the cause (Table A pressure) already happened many steps ago
- Downstream observers are now reacting to what upstream observers already knew

**The shadow appears.** By the time Table B shows momentum, Table A pressure has already resolved.

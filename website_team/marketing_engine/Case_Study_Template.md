## 1. Executive Summary (200 words max)

**One-paragraph hook that answers:**
- What was the client's core problem?
- What solution did we implement?
- What was the outcome (in numbers)?

**Template:**

[Client name], a [industry] company generating [revenue/scale], faced a critical challenge: [core problem]. Their existing [old solution/status quo] was resulting in [negative outcome: lost revenue, poor conversion, high bounce rate, etc.]. We partnered to redesign [what we changed] and implement [key solutions]. Within [timeframe], they achieved [primary metric]: [X% improvement] in [KPI], resulting in [business impact: revenue, leads, cost savings]. This case study outlines our approach, key decisions, and measurable results.

**Example:**

TechFlow Marketing, a mid-market B2B SaaS agency, struggled with website conversion rates stuck at 1.2%. Their homepage failed to communicate value clearly, and visitors bounced without understanding their service offerings. We restructured their website architecture, rewrote all copy around core customer pain points, and implemented a strategic landing page testing program. Within 90 days, conversion rates improved to 3.8%—a 217% increase—resulting in an additional $180K in annual recurring revenue (ARR) from web alone.

## 2. The Challenge (300-400 words)

**What was the situation before we got involved?**

### 2.1 Client Background
- Company size, industry, revenue stage
- What they do (products/services)
- Who their customers are (target market)
- Market position (leader, challenger, established, growing)

### 2.2 The Core Problem
**Be specific. Not "poor design" but:**
- Conversion rate stuck at X%
- Bounce rate consistently above Y%
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC) at $Z (unsustainably high)
- Specific pages underperforming (homepage, pricing, checkout)
- User confusion around value proposition
- Lack of trust signals / social proof
- High cart abandonment (e-commerce)
- Lead quality issues (getting volume, not qualified leads)

### 2.3 Impact of the Problem
- Revenue lost (quantify: $X/month, Y% of potential revenue)
- Customer acquisition becoming more expensive
- Competitive disadvantage in their market
- Team frustration / inability to scale sales
- Specific customer feedback ("customers say...," "surveys show...")

### 2.4 Why Previous Attempts Failed
- Had they tried to fix it themselves? What happened?
- Hired another agency before? Why didn't it work?
- Internal team constraints (lack of expertise, time, resources)
- Previous approaches missed the root cause

### 2.5 Why They Chose Us
- What made them decide to work with Sentinel Labs?
- What were they looking for in a partner?
- Any competitive context (why us vs. alternatives)?

**Template Paragraph:**

TechFlow had built a solid product but couldn't communicate it effectively online. Their website looked professional on the surface, but user testing revealed critical gaps: visitors didn't understand what TechFlow actually did, how their services differed from competitors, or what success looked like for a client. The result: a 1.2% conversion rate on paid traffic, with a CAC of $850 on a $3,200 average contract value—an unsustainable 3.7:1 ratio. Their sales team reported spending hours qualifying inbound leads that weren't actually a fit. TechFlow knew they needed a strategic redesign, not just a fresh coat of paint.

## 3. Our Approach (400-500 words)

**What did we actually do?**

### 3.1 Discovery & Strategy Phase
- How did we diagnose the problem?
  - User research (interviews, surveys, heatmaps)
  - Competitive analysis (what are competitors doing?)
  - Data analysis (traffic sources, funnel dropoff, behavioral patterns)
  - Stakeholder interviews (what does the team know about customer pain points?)

**Example:** "We conducted 8 in-depth customer interviews, analyzed 3 months of Google Analytics data, and reviewed heatmaps from 10K+ sessions. This revealed that 67% of visitors couldn't articulate the difference between TechFlow and competitors within 30 seconds on the homepage."

### 3.2 Strategic Recommendations
- What did we recommend?
  - Website architecture changes (reorganize pages, simplify navigation)
  - Messaging/copy overhaul (new value proposition, clearer CTAs)
  - Visual design shifts (trustworthiness, clarity, brand alignment)
  - Functional additions (lead magnet, trust signals, social proof)
  - Specific features/tools we added

**Example:** "We recommended a complete homepage rebuild focused on three changes: (1) A new hero section leading with customer pain point, not product features; (2) Three benefit-focused sections replacing the old feature-dump approach; (3) A strategic lead magnet ('Free Conversion Audit') to qualify inbound before sales outreach."

### 3.3 Implementation & Execution
- How did we build it?
  - Technology stack / tools used
  - Team composition (designers, copy writers, developers)
  - Timeline (how long for each phase: design, copy, development, testing)
  - Any obstacles we overcame
  - How we involved the client (collaboration points, review cycles)

**Example:** "Over 12 weeks, our team (designer + copywriter + developer) created 15+ design iterations, tested 6 headline variations, and built a custom lead qualification form. We involved TechFlow stakeholders in weekly reviews, ensuring we stayed aligned with their brand voice and strategic goals."

### 3.4 Testing & Optimization
- Did we A/B test anything?
- Did we measure and iterate?
- What changed based on early data?

**Example:** "During the first month post-launch, we ran three headline A/B tests. The original headline performed well, but a problem-focused variant ('Stop Losing $50K/Month to Conversion Leaks') outperformed it by 34%. We implemented this variant site-wide."

## 5. Key Learnings & Methodology (250-300 words)

**What did we learn? What would we do differently? Why does this matter?**

### 5.1 Strategic Insights
- What surprised us about this project?
- What customer/market insight did we uncover?
- How did this change our thinking?

**Example:**
"This project reinforced a critical insight: **messaging beats design every time.** TechFlow's old website was well-designed but communicated nothing about their core value. Once we aligned copy with customer pain points, design improvements became secondary. This principle now shapes every web project we undertake."

### 5.2 Tactical Wins
- What specific tactics worked?
- Why do we think they worked?
- Where else would this approach apply?

**Example:**
"The problem-focused hero statement ('Stop losing $50K/month to conversion leaks') significantly outperformed benefit-focused alternatives. This taught us to lead with customer pain, not product benefits—a principle that now drives all our homepage copy."

### 5.3 Challenges & Solutions
- What went wrong?
- How did we adapt?
- What would we do differently next time?

**Example:**
"Initially, we over-complicated the service offering. After user testing revealed confusion, we simplified the navigation from 7 services to 3 core services (with a 'view all' option). This taught us: **complexity is the enemy of conversion.** We now ruthlessly simplify site architecture on every project."

### 5.4 Methodology Application
Explain why this methodology matters for other similar clients:

"This approach—combining user research, competitive analysis, strategic messaging, and data-driven testing—is now our standard operating procedure for any website redesign. Whether the client is B2B SaaS, professional services, or e-commerce, this framework produces results."

## 7. Metrics & ROI Calculation (150-200 words)

**Show your math. How do we calculate the ROI?**

### Simple ROI Formula:
```
(Revenue Gained - Project Cost) / Project Cost × 100 = ROI %

Example:
($180K annual revenue gain - $42.5K project cost) / $42.5K × 100 = 323% ROI
```

### Payback Period:
```
Project Cost / Monthly Revenue Gain = Months to Break Even

Example:
$42.5K / $15K per month = 2.8 months to full payback
```

### Long-Term Value (Year 1):
```
Annual Revenue Gain - Project Cost = Net Benefit

Example:
$180K - $42.5K = $137.5K net benefit in Year 1
```

**Be Transparent:**
- If the project took longer than expected, explain why
- If results took time to materialize, be honest about timeline
- If there were trade-offs, discuss them

Example: "The full 217% conversion improvement took 90 days to stabilize. In month 1, we saw 50% improvement; month 2, 140%; month 3, plateau at 217%. This is typical as we test and iterate."

## FAQ: Building Your Case Study Library

**Q: How many case studies do I need?**  
A: Start with 3-5 strong ones covering different service types/industries. Aim for 10-15 within your first year.

**Q: Should I anonymize clients?**  
A: Use real names when possible (stronger proof). Anonymize only if NDA requires it.

**Q: What if results weren't dramatic?**  
A: Tell the truth. A 35% improvement is still worth documenting. Varied results prove you work across different contexts.

**Q: How long should a case study be?**  
A: 2,000-3,000 words for full version. Create a 500-word summary for social/sales use.

**Q: When should I publish?**  
A: After 60-90 days post-project (enough time to verify results are sustained).
